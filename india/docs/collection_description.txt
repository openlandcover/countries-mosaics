IOLN ANNUAL LANDSAT MOSAICS OF INDIA, VERSION 2

WHAT THIS IS
One finished satellite image of one part of India for one year, built
by combining every usable Landsat picture taken that year. There is one
image for each of 283 grid cells of the 1:250,000 map sheet series, for
each year from 1986 to 2025. The year is the phenological year, 1 April
to 31 March, so that both of India's crop seasons sit inside one image;
the label is the starting year, so 2019 means April 2019 to March 2020.
Pixels are 30 m, nominally: the grid is in degrees (EPSG:4326), so a
pixel is about 30 m and not exactly 30 m. Made by the India Open
LandCover Network. It follows the earlier India mosaic series, which
remains available as version 1.

HOW IT WAS MADE
Landsat Collection 2 surface reflectance, every satellite flying that
year. Cloud and shadow removed by three checks: the archive's own
flags, a temperature check (cloud is colder than the ground it hides),
and a brightness cut for cloud the flags missed. Hillside lighting
divided out using physics rather than a fit. Sun and view angles
normalised (BRDF). Older satellites converted onto the Landsat 8 and 9
basis. Then, for each pixel and each layer, the year is summarised:
a typical value for the whole year, one for its greenest quarter, one
for its least-green quarter, the change between those two, and a
robust measure of spread.

READING THE NUMBERS
Every value is stored as a whole number and must be decoded first.
  reflectance bands      true value = stored x 0.0001
  surface temperature    kelvin = stored x 0.1
  ground-cover shares    the stored number IS the percentage
  index bands            true value = stored x 0.0001, never shifted
  ndfi, bci, ibi levels  true value = stored / 100 - 1
  their spread and swing true value = stored / 100
  terrain               slope = stored / 100 degrees; height above the
                        nearest stream = stored / 10 metres; elevation
                        in whole metres
  longitude, latitude    degrees = stored / 10000
  count bands            plain whole numbers, no conversion
Every image also carries these formulas as its own properties.

BEFORE YOU USE IT
Some values are labels, not measurements. In the ndfi level and spread
bands, -10 means the pixel is lasting water and -20 means lasting snow;
in the ndfi swing band, -999 means there was no real pair of seasonal
values to subtract. Remove these before averaging, rescaling or fitting
anything. A decision tree can use them as they stand.
The two aspect bands are damped by slope and are meant to be used as
they are; never rebuild a compass direction from them.
The count bands say how much evidence sits under a pixel. Read them to
judge how far to trust a value, but never train a model on them: they
record the history of the satellite archive, not the ground.
Longitude and latitude are meant to be used as inputs.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO WELL
Before 2000 the archive is thin, and in places a pixel-year rests on
one or two observations; the count bands say where. The temperature
check cannot run in those years, so every cloud flag is upheld, which
is the cautious choice. Cell-years with no usable imagery are left out
rather than filled, so gaps in the record are real. Landsat 7 after
2003 has blank stripes; they are filled only where nothing else was
available, and faint marks remain in 2012. In steep wet forest the
terrain correction overshoots slightly in the near-infrared.
